# Banned from fanedit.org

Flashback continues here. The old venue does not.

They **never provide ban reasons**. They **never provide shadowban reasons**. The act is to look like staff are only **curating inappropriate comments** — while they **archive entire projects** because **two words** showed up in a description body. That is not moderation. That is soft erasure with a manners costume.

The last thing I told them before the ban: **post your rules**. If a term should be avoided or prohibited, **say so**. Anyone who once saw the public Wikipedia pages on the subject has no reason to assume those words are now unspeakable on a film forum. Silence is the trap — and I walked into it because I never realized the vocabulary had been banned at all.

We later found **two** Wikipedia entries for predictive programming deleted — **2015**, then **2020**. 2020 opened a brutal stretch of American cancel culture. Scrubbing the last encyclopedia page that still existed that year set the **grounds for removal** anywhere for anyone who still treated the term as live. Words so forbidden they cannot appear in site rules are how you know you are in a **black hole**, not a craft community with posted guidelines.

That is not a nonpartisan manners dispute. In that era it was overwhelmingly one political-cultural machine that made canceling people strategy at every social level, while accusing the other side of the same — treating attacks on a president or on Christian faith as normal, then calling it “cancel culture” when friends finally stopped listening. **Real cancel culture** is unposted rules, shadow removal without notice, and no audience left to petition.

From beginning to end, **disingenuous**. That is why I read the venue as **mostly industry-agent run**. Ownership norms on the label; kill-switches in the back office. This repository is the public catalog now. Full analysis on the [Why](/motivation) page.
